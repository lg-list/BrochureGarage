INFINITI · AUTOMOTIVE HISTORY

Infiniti

A Japanese luxury brand created specifically for the North American market and developed around performance, design and premium technology.

01 · ORIGINS

Where the Story Begins

Infiniti was created by Nissan in 1989, alongside the launch of other Japanese luxury brands in North America. Its mission was to give Nissan a dedicated premium identity rather than compete solely under the parent brand.

04 · EDITORIAL ANALYSIS

A Luxury Brand Designed Around a Different Audience

Infiniti was created to give Nissan a dedicated premium identity in North America. Its early products combined Japanese engineering with rear-wheel-drive performance, quietness and a more distinctive design language. The challenge was not simply building good cars; it was convincing buyers that a new badge deserved to sit beside established luxury marques.

The G35 and FX were especially important because they broadened Infiniti’s identity beyond large luxury sedans. Performance sedans, coupes and crossovers gave the brand a more dynamic personality and helped define the modern premium-market formula in which driving character and design matter as much as traditional luxury equipment.

06 · CORPORATE HISTORY

Ownership, Scale and Strategy

Infiniti is Nissan's luxury brand and has repeatedly adjusted its model range and naming system to compete across North America, China, the Middle East and other premium markets.

07 · INDUSTRY IMPACT

Why Infiniti Matters

Infiniti helped establish the Japanese luxury-brand model in the United States and contributed to the development of the luxury performance crossover.

EDITORIAL ANALYSIS

What the Infiniti Story Reveals

A brand history is more useful when it explains why the important decisions mattered.

Infiniti was created to give Nissan a dedicated premium identity, particularly in North America, where Japanese manufacturers were competing directly with established luxury marques. Rear-wheel-drive platforms, distinctive styling and advanced technology became important parts of the proposition.

The brand's history shows both the opportunity and difficulty of premium sub-brands. Creating a separate badge can help a manufacturer reposition products, but long-term differentiation requires a coherent design language, product cadence and customer experience. Infiniti's evolution illustrates those constraints clearly.

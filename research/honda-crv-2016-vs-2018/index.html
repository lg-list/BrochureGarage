2016 vs 2018 Honda CR-V: What the Brochures Actually Show

A comparison of two U.S. sales brochures, with exact PDF page references. Reviewed October 13, 2026.

The badge on the tailgate is an incomplete description of a used CR-V. Two vehicles advertised as EX can have different engines and driver-assistance equipment depending on the model year. Reading the equipment ladders in these two brochures resolves that ambiguity before a buyer relies on a short listing description.

The documents and comparison method

We visually reviewed the archived U.S. 2016 and 2018 Honda CR-V brochures. The files contain 11 and 10 PDF pages respectively. Both files are image-based: text search alone will not locate the equipment table. In this article, page numbers refer to the PDF viewer's page counter, including the cover, rather than printed page numbers.

The table below uses the trim panels on 2016 brochure, PDF page 10 and 2018 brochure, PDF page 10. It compares what each document states. It does not test the vehicle, verify the condition of a used example, or describe every market.

The engine difference depends on trim

The 2016 panel begins with the LX's 2.4-liter engine and then presents each higher trim as additions or replacements. The 2018 panel separates the LX's 184-hp engine from the EX's 190-hp turbo engine. The useful research result is not simply that a newer year has more power: the engine distinction applies to a specific trim step. A 2018 LX and a 2018 EX should not be treated as interchangeable in an engine comparison.

Follow the equipment ladder

In the 2016 document the SE sits between LX and EX; the 2018 ladder moves directly from LX to EX. The Honda Sensing suite appears in the 2018 EX additions, while the named braking, lane-warning, lane-keeping and cruise features appear in the 2016 Touring additions. That changes which panel a reader needs to inspect when checking an advertised feature.

Reading only the marketing spread is insufficient. 2018 brochure, PDF page 8 explains assistance functions, but page 10 identifies the trim step. The practical sequence is to find the feature description, then locate its availability in the trim ladder, then read the footnote. A photograph of a high-specification vehicle does not establish standard equipment on the entry trim.

Colors and upholstery need a separate check

The two page-10 spreads also contain color and interior panels. Treat those as another comparison layer instead of using the body-color photograph to identify a trim. Record the color name, interior material, and any trim restriction together. The 2016 document includes trim-combination tables, whereas the 2018 spread groups its interior swatches by trim family. A listing that says only "leather" omits the year-specific context needed to verify it.

A repeatable used-car research checklist

  1. Match model year, country and exact trim before comparing an engine rating.
  2. Read the entry-trim panel and the additions for the advertised trim together.
  3. Record the page for each equipment claim and keep footnotes with the claim.
  4. Confirm installed equipment with the vehicle's records and inspection; the brochure describes an offering, not an individual car.

For another year or an alternate edition, open the CR-V collection or find CR-V documents. Current recalls and safety status require current official sources; these period brochures are not a current safety assessment.
